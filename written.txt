a. Insert p_k = (2^-k, 2^-k) for k = 1..n. Every split leaves only one point
   out and pushes the rest into the top-left child, so the tree becomes a
   chain of depth ~n (a list).
   Constant time: query (0.9, 0.9). It falls in the root's bottom-right child,
   which is empty, so we stop right away.
   O(n) time: query (0, 0) (or any point very close to it). We have to walk
   down the whole chain to reach it.

b. The node keeps splitting forever. All the copies always fall in the same
   child, so the leaf never gets under capacity and we recurse until the
   floats run out or the stack overflows.
   Fix: cap the tree depth (or a minimum cell size) and let leaves at that
   limit go over capacity. Another option is to store duplicates once with
   a count.

c. A quadtree splits fixed space, not the objects. Translating or rotating
   the scene moves objects across cell boundaries (rotation also changes
   their AABBs), so the tree has to be mostly rebuilt. Quadtrees work well
   for static or slowly changing scenes inside known bounds. They are bad
   for scenes with large or global movement, where a BVH that can just be
   refit works better.
